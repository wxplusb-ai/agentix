# Estimation Discovery Session

How to run an estimation discovery session, handle uncertainty, and judge when the answers are enough to plan.

## Before the session

- Send the client the questions in advance, grouped by topic rather than by node, so they can gather numbers and bring the right people.
- Know which answers matter most: questions feeding the largest tasks and the nodes that test top bets deserve the most care.

## Running it

- Work topic by topic. Explain briefly why each group of questions matters ("these tell us how big the data preparation work is").
- Ask for counts and observable facts, not opinions about effort. The client knows how many document types they have; estimating hours is the Analyst's job.
- When an answer surprises you, ask how they know. A surprising number is often a belief, and may belong in Beliefs and Bets.
- Capture who answered and how confident they were.

## When the client does not know

"I don't know" is not an answer the plan can use, but a guess presented as fact is worse. Instead:

1. Ask for a range: "What is the smallest it could plausibly be? The largest?"
2. Ask what the range depends on. The answer often reveals a belief worth testing.
3. Record the low, the high and the reasoning.

If even a range is impossible, the node may be outside the planning horizon: it cannot be estimated until something earlier is learned. Tell the Analyst; the node may move to node-level planning, or the question may become a bet.

## When the answers are enough

The answers are sufficient when:

- Every question for every task-level node has a value or a reasoned range.
- Every node-level node can be placed relative to its worked examples.
- Scheduling inputs are known: start date, people, weekly hours, working days, known waits.
- No answer contradicts an approved deliverable without the contradiction being resolved.
