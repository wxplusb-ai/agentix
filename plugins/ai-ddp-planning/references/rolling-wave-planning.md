# Rolling-Wave Planning

How much detail each part of an AI-AD&D plan carries, and why.

## Planning horizons

Rolling-wave planning (progressive elaboration) plans in detail as far as the team can see, and in outline beyond that. The distance the team can see is the planning horizon. It may be a number of weeks, the next phase or the next milestone. As work proceeds, unknowns are resolved and the next stretch can be planned in detail with real confidence.

Knutson's mountain climber cannot say exactly how long the whole climb will take on a mountain never seen before. The climber can give a range for the whole climb and a detailed plan for getting the party to the base and up the first stage.

## Approximating versus estimating

- **An estimate** is a number backed by known scope and answered questions. It belongs inside the planning horizon.
- **An approximation** is a range backed by experience and comparable work. It belongs beyond the horizon. It is stated as a range ("140 to 220 hours"), never a single confident figure, and labeled as an approximation.

## The three tiers

An AI-AD&D plan has three levels of detail, matching the plan hierarchy:

| Tier | Where it applies | What the plan contains |
|---|---|---|
| Task | The nodes inside the planning horizon: the current nodes and perhaps the next | Each task from the catalog, adapted to the project, with an estimate from its estimation guideline, start and end dates, priority and tags |
| Node | Nodes just beyond the horizon | One row per node with an approximate range and tentative dates; tasks not yet broken out |
| Phase | Later phases | One row per phase with an approximate range and an approximate window |

Where the horizon falls is a judgment for the Analyst, reviewed by the Team Lead. Inside the horizon, every estimate must rest on an answered question. If a node cannot be estimated from what is known, it belongs outside the horizon.

## The plan is re-run, not maintained by the agents

The client decides when to plan again, typically at a milestone, when a bet has been tested. The process then runs again on the in-flight project: the Overview captures what was completed and learned, the bets are updated, and the next stretch is elaborated to task level.
